Leisure — Full Poem

— W.H. Davies —
WHAT is this life if, full of care,
We have no time to stand and stare?
No time to stand beneath the boughs,
And stare as long as sheep and cows:
No time to see, when woods we pass,
Where squirrels hide their nuts in grass:
No time to see, in broad daylight,
Streams full of stars, like skies at night:
No time to turn at Beauty's glance,
And watch her feet, how they can dance:
No time to wait till her mouth can,
Enrich that smile her eyes began?
A poor life this if, full of care,
We have no time to stand and stare.

Overall Symbolic Interpretation

This poem symbolically contrasts two ways of living:

  • Animals (sheep, cows, squirrels) → live freely, slowly, and joyfully in nature
  • Humans → are trapped in a busy, careless rush through life

"Beauty" is personified as a dancing woman whose smile begins in her eyes. This symbolises Nature herself — graceful, generous, and always present — but ignored by busy humans. The stream full of stars is a symbol of the hidden magic of the natural world — visible only to those who pause and look. The poem ultimately symbolises that a life without leisure and mindful observation is a "poor life" — spiritually and emotionally empty.

Teacher's Speech Notes
A ready-to-use explanation for the classroom

"Good morning, class! Today we are going to read a beautiful poem called 'Leisure' written by W.H. Davies. Before we begin, let me ask you a simple question — when was the last time you sat quietly and just looked at the sky, a river, or some birds? Most of us would say — 'Not recently!' — and that is exactly what this poem is about."

"W.H. Davies was a Welsh poet who lived a very unusual life. He was once a wanderer — a man who travelled on foot with no fixed home. Because of this life, he learned to notice small, beautiful things that busy people overlook. He wrote this poem to remind us that life is not only about work — it is also about seeing, feeling, and enjoying what is around us."

"The title 'Leisure' means 'फुर्सत' — free time. But Davies is asking — do we actually use our free time? Do we really pause and enjoy life?"

"The poem begins with a powerful question: 'What is this life if, full of care, we have no time to stand and stare?' This means — what is the value of our life if we are always so busy and worried that we cannot even stop to look at the world around us?"

"Davies then gives us several beautiful images from nature — tree branches, squirrels hiding nuts, streams reflecting stars, and a beautiful woman dancing. All these things are around us every day — but we are too busy to notice them."

"The poem ends by repeating the opening question — making it clear that a life without leisure — without pausing to appreciate beauty — is a POOR life."

"So, class, the message is simple but powerful: slow down, look around, and enjoy the small beautiful moments of life. Let us now read the poem together..."

Line-by-Line Explanation
Understanding each stanza of the poem
Stanza 1
"What is this life if, full of care, / We have no time to stand and stare?"
Explanation: The poet opens with a rhetorical question. He asks — what kind of life are we living if we are so busy and worried (full of care) that we cannot even stop (stand) and look (stare) at the world around us? The word "care" means worries and responsibilities. This sets the central theme: modern humans are too busy to enjoy life.
Stanza 2
"No time to stand beneath the boughs, / And stare as long as sheep and cows:"
Explanation: Here the poet says we have no time to stand under the branches of trees (boughs) and simply watch — the way sheep and cows do. The animals are used as a positive example — they live slowly, peacefully, and enjoy their surroundings. Humans, ironically, are busier and less peaceful than animals.
Stanza 3
"No time to see, when woods we pass, / Where squirrels hide their nuts in grass:"
Explanation: When we walk through the forest (woods), we are in such a hurry that we do not even notice the small, delightful activities happening around us — like squirrels hiding their food in the grass. These tiny scenes of nature are precious but we miss them completely.
Stanza 4
"No time to see, in broad daylight, / Streams full of stars, like skies at night:"
Explanation: This is one of the most poetic and visual images in the poem. During the daytime, when sunlight hits a stream, the rippling water looks like it is full of stars — just like the night sky. But we never pause to see this magical sight. The comparison (simile) here — streams like skies — highlights the extraordinary beauty available in ordinary moments.
Stanza 5
"No time to turn at Beauty's glance, / And watch her feet, how they can dance:"
Explanation: "Beauty" is here personified as a graceful woman. When Beauty glances at us (offers us a beautiful moment), we don't even turn to look. We certainly don't watch how her feet dance — i.e., how gracefully nature moves and flows around us. The poet mourns that we ignore these beautiful invitations.
Stanza 6
"No time to wait till her mouth can, / Enrich that smile her eyes began?"
Explanation: This continues the personification of Beauty. When a person smiles, the smile begins in the eyes before it reaches the mouth. The poet says we are so rushed that we cannot even wait for Beauty's full smile to appear — we miss even the complete expression of natural beauty. This is a deeply tender image of what hurry costs us.
Stanza 7 (Final)
"A poor life this if, full of care, / We have no time to stand and stare."
Explanation: The poem ends by repeating the opening idea — but now it is not a question, it is a conclusion. The poet declares firmly: if we are so full of care and worry that we have no time to pause and observe beauty, then we are living a poor life — not poor in money, but poor in experience, joy, and meaning. This repetition (refrain) makes the message unforgettable.
