📖 The Rautes — Reading Text

Paragraph I
The Rautes (राउटे) are one of the most typical indigenous groups of Nepal sustaining their unique cultural identities for generations. They are the only nomadic people in the country who never settle permanently in any particular place. The Rautes deny any idea on permanent settlement, education or agriculture. The fact that the Rautes, the last fulltime nomads of Nepal, have survived into this century is truly remarkable in our current period of diminishing cultural diversity.
Paragraph II
It is estimated that the total population of Rautes in Nepal is about 180, or roughly 52 families. The Karnali Province of Nepal remains their only sanctuary.
Paragraph III
There is no documented history of the Rautes. But one common ground is that once they fled from the state, they never returned and established a different pattern of life in the jungle. To eke out their living, they mastered crafting of wooden products, learned to hunt monkeys, and adapted to forest life.
Paragraph IV
Only the Mukhiya of the Rautes, on behalf of their tribe, talks to outsiders regarding their lifestyle and tradition.
Paragraph V
The Rautes bear traditional knowledge of living in a community. The heredity of the Raute is categorised into three types as Kalyaal, Raskoti and Samaal. It is therefore very important to note the various aspects of these people, such as their ethical norms and values, rituals and livelihood patterns.
Paragraph VI
At the time when acculturation has been widespread in the world with diminishing diversity, the Rautes have been quite successful in sustaining their tradition. Humanity has to learn a lot from the Rautes, since they have been very successful in preserving their tradition in today's world. Their maintenance of the nomadic culture in a rapidly globalising world is commendable. This global uniqueness makes them a precious part of the diverse people of Nepal.
Paragraph VII
Like any other ethnic group following the Hindu society, the Raute community also maintains a patriarchal social hierarchy. However, in matters of internal management, women have a more prominent role than that of their male counterparts.
Paragraph VIII
The Rautes maintain a clear division of labour between men and women. Most often the male members get involved in making wooden utensils, hunting monkeys, trading their products in the market, and collecting food grains. It is also common for unmarried girls and widows to go to the countryside and collect food grains and deliver the order for the craft items. Women in the Raute community bear the major responsibility in the household chores and dominate in almost every facet of the livelihood except in the areas of socialising, hunting, carpentry, and dancing. They have the indigenous know-how on spring water sources. Since it is a part of their job to search for medicinal herbs, firewood and vegetables in the jungle, they also have extensive knowledge of the forest and its ecosystem.
Paragraph IX
The husband and wife have great respect and love for each other. Married women never travel outside of their settlement area with anyone else other than their husbands. The women are mainly responsible for collecting firewood, cooking meals and rearing their children. They also may assist the males while shifting homes to new locations and building new homes there. Children until the age of 10–14 are not assigned any major household responsibilities; they only spend their days playing with natural objects and roaming around the settlement. On special occasions, they may help their parents in fetching water from the spring and carrying small items while shifting to new locations.
Paragraph X
Monkey hunting is considered fundamental to maintain the cultural permanence of the hunting life of the Rautes. Their hunting technique is also very rare in the sense that around 8–30 young and middle aged Raute members go hunting in a group with their nets. They do not use guns or bows and arrows for hunting.
Paragraph XI
The Rautes observe the festivals like Saune Sankranti, Dashain, Tihar, Chaite Dashain and Maghe Sankranti. For the celebrations, the Rautes manage food, homemade ale and meat in advance. As they enjoy complete participation in the community events, they do not like to meet strangers or travel beyond their settlement area during their celebration period. They take complete leave from carpentry and hunting during the festivals. These festivals are observed with much joy and fervour amidst singing, dancing, feasting and their own ritual performances. Rice, meat and drinks play an important role in their feast.

✅ Exercise B — True or False

a. The Rautes move from one place to another for their settlements. ▶
✅ True — The Rautes are nomadic people who never settle permanently.
b. Rautes live in the far-western part of Nepal. ▶
✅ True — The Karnali Province (which includes far-western areas) is their only sanctuary.
c. One of the means of their survival is woodcraft. ▶
✅ True — They master crafting wooden products to eke out their living.
d. The maintenance of the nomadic culture is highly regarded. ▶
✅ True — Their maintenance of nomadic culture in a globalising world is commendable.
e. The Raute community is female-dominated. ▶
❌ False — The Raute community is patriarchal (male-dominated), though women have a prominent role in internal management.

✅ Exercise C — Answer the Following Questions

a. What is the common belief about the history of the Rautes? ▶
The common belief is that once the Rautes fled from the state, they never returned and established a different pattern of life in the jungle. There is no documented history of the Rautes.
b. How do Rautes earn their living? ▶
The Rautes earn their living by crafting wooden products, hunting monkeys, trading their wooden products in the market, and collecting food grains.
c. How do Raute children spend their days? ▶
Raute children until the age of 10–14 are not assigned any major household responsibilities. They spend their days playing with natural objects and roaming around the settlement. On special occasions, they help their parents fetch water and carry small items while shifting to new locations.
d. Why do the Rautes have good knowledge of the forest and its ecosystem? ▶
Because it is part of the women's job to search for medicinal herbs, firewood, and vegetables in the jungle, they develop extensive knowledge of the forest and its ecosystem.
e. Is the task division between male and female Rautes fair? Why? ▶
The task division is based on traditional roles. Men do hunting, carpentry, trading, and social activities while women manage household chores, child-rearing, and collecting forest resources. Though both contribute equally, some may argue it is not fully fair as women bear the greater burden of household responsibilities.
f. What is special about the hunting skills of the Rautes? ▶
The Rautes hunt monkeys in groups of 8–30 using nets. They do not use guns, bows, or arrows, which makes their hunting technique unique and traditional.
g. Name the festivals that the Rautes celebrate. ▶
The Rautes celebrate Saune Sankranti, Dashain, Tihar, Chaite Dashain, and Maghe Sankranti.
h. What should modern people learn from the Rautes? ▶
Modern people should learn how to preserve their cultural identity and traditions even in a rapidly globalising world. The Rautes have successfully sustained their nomadic culture, which is commendable and inspiring.

➕ Short Questions

1. Who are the Rautes and where do they live? ▶
The Rautes are a nomadic indigenous group of Nepal who live in the Karnali Province. They never settle permanently in one place.
2. What are the three hereditary categories of the Raute community? ▶
The three hereditary categories of the Raute community are Kalyaal, Raskoti, and Samaal.
3. How do women contribute to the Raute community's livelihood? ▶
Women manage household chores, rear children, collect firewood, search for medicinal herbs and vegetables in the jungle, and assist in shifting homes to new locations.
4. Why is monkey hunting important for the Rautes? ▶
Monkey hunting is considered fundamental to maintaining the cultural permanence of the hunting life of the Rautes.
5. How do the Rautes celebrate their festivals? ▶
The Rautes celebrate their festivals with singing, dancing, feasting, and ritual performances. They prepare food, homemade ale, and meat in advance and avoid meeting strangers during this period.

➕ True / False

1. The Rautes are the only fulltime nomadic people in Nepal. ▶
✅ True — They are the last fulltime nomads of Nepal.
2. The total population of Rautes is about 500 families. ▶
❌ False — The Raute population is about 180 people, or roughly 52 families (not 500).
3. Women in the Raute community are responsible for making wooden utensils. ▶
❌ False — Making wooden utensils is the male task; women handle household chores and forest resource collection.
4. The Rautes use guns to hunt monkeys. ▶
❌ False — The Rautes use nets, not guns, bows, or arrows, to hunt monkeys.
5. The Rautes take complete leave from carpentry and hunting during festivals. ▶
✅ True — During festival periods, they stop all carpentry and hunting work.
